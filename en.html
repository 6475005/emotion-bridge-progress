HUMAN–ROBOT INTERACTION · 2026.09.14

Qwen × body generation.
Inside the model interface.

A frozen dialogue/speech model and a speech-conditioned body diffusion model. A technical report on two separate models connected sequentially through generated-audio features.

Japanese + EnglishQwen3-Omni + learned bodySota experiment preparation
Verified: sequential generation and synchronized playback. Physical and participant evaluation remain open.
Qwen completes the reply audio before the body model generates its movement. This does not establish simultaneous speech/body generation or joint end-to-end training.

01 / MODEL ARCHITECTURE

Two models.
One sequential pipeline.

This is an audio-mediated two-model cascade. The encoder is an existing Qwen component; the body diffusion model has separate parameters and training losses.

How integration works

Qwen and the body model stay in one resident worker, joined by reply identity and exact audio hashes. The existing audio encoder is reused to avoid duplicate GPU loading. Stages execute sequentially within a request, then speech and movement play together.

Inside the body model

A width256,12-block shared lineage uses source-specific input/output projections and motion codecs. The adopted v5 has14,055,926 trainable body parameters; this is not the total including Qwen.

Training and normalization details

Japanese/ZEGGS predict latent dynamics; Trinity predicts pose and dynamics. Training combines diffusion prediction loss with decoded-motion objectives through frozen decoders. Generated histories are conditions, never replacement teachers. Statistics use fitting splits; Qwen, the original English route and codecs remain frozen during continuation.

Trinity8k→16k continuation: batches32 Japanese,16 ZEGGS,16 Trinity; source weights1:0.25:0.25. Existing AdamW states are restored; only10 new Trinity tensors start with empty optimizer state. Generated-history mixture0.5, audio/history dropout0.1. These describe the executed lineage, not a new ongoing training run.

01B / MOTION LEARNING & REVIEW

Learn from human motion.
Compare, then decide.

Training uses recorded speech paired with human movement. Deployment passes actual Qwen reply audio through the same feature pathway. The adopted lineage is distinguished here from subsequent unpromoted candidates.

  1. 01

    Prepare paired speech and human movement

    Preserve source IDs, clocks, rigs and exclusions. Split recordings into fitting/development sets, then form4-second windows.

  2. 02

    Frozen features and motion representations

    Qwen audio features:52×2048. Source-specific codecs encode movement; normalization uses fitting-only statistics.

  3. 03

    Train speech-conditioned body diffusion

    Add noise to teacher motion codes and predict v using audio, history and diffusion time. Update shared blocks and rig-specific projections; keep Qwen and codecs frozen.

  4. 04

    Controlled continuation from complete state

    Restore complete16k weights, AdamW and RNG states. Compare control and intervention with matched rows/noise. Generated history is a condition, not a replacement for observed teachers.

  5. 05

    Decide using metrics and playback

    Review pose, temporal change, seams, diversity and actual replies. Training loss or smoothness alone is insufficient. The original v5 remains the live model.

What a training batch optimizes

teacher motion → frozen codec → normalized clean code z
x_t = a(t) · z + b(t) · noise
v_target = a(t) · noise − b(t) · z
v_pred = body(x_t, audio_features, history, t)
clean_estimate = a(t) · x_t − b(t) · v_pred
decoded_motion = frozen_decoder(denormalize(clean_estimate))

Source review: speech, body and transcript intervals

Review retains boundary-spanning text, no-transcript windows and empty-text rows. Missing transcript does not mean silence or no motion. No invented subword times, physical synchrony or semantic alignment claims.

Original audio/motion arrays are not published; sources are the Aud2Repr2Pose author archive and GENEA2023 below. Software playback checks do not substitute for human ratings or successful physical Sota use.

02 / INPUT → OUTPUT

What goes in,
and what comes out?

Example input

“I struggled with my presentation today and feel a little down.”
Verified path: text, actual dialogue history and explicit language. Audio/image input exists in the foundation but remains unverified through the latest body integration.

Example output

Reply text, Qwen-native speech and a30 Hz motion sequence. These currently play together in a browser schematic. Physical execution requires joint mapping and controller output.
The pictured head/arm gestures are illustrative, not guaranteed semantic behaviors.

Intended scenarios are short everyday exchanges: disappointment after a presentation, satisfaction after finishing work, or plans for tomorrow. No clinical or counselling effect is claimed.

Counting correctly

GENEA’s nominal18.32 hours count both role channels, not18.32 hours of independent conversation. Full speaker/content/session independence across datasets is unestablished. These are development records, not untouched test results.

The experiment needs elapsed time from the end of the user’s utterance to the first audible robot reply. The recorded2.09–3.04 s starts at text-button submission and ends at first browser audio-clock progress. It measures neither end-of-utterance detection nor loudspeaker onset and is not the conversational reply latency.

06 / RESEARCH CONTRIBUTION

Contribution depends on
what the study establishes.

The intended contribution is not a world-first speech/motion generator, fastest latency or highest realism. It is learned human-motion transfer integrated with Japanese/English dialogue, its expression on Sota, and evaluation of how people experience it.

AspectCurrent positionEvidence still needed
Integrated generationSequential processing in one resident workerSimultaneous generation / causal streaming is a separate unimplemented task
LatencyConversational reply latency unmeasured; four instrumented button-submit examplesPhysical latency distribution, failure rate and matched comparison
RealismQuality sufficient for a short robot experimentPhysical playback and human judgments
Benefit of learningStatic control prepared to test motion presenceMoving procedural baseline to test a method advantage
HRI contributionAffinity, felt understanding and willingness to continue are hypothesesPilot and independent evaluation with a frozen protocol

Motion-Omni reports motion from speech-producing hidden states. Our current system feeds completed audio to a body model, so these are different meanings of integration. Distinctions and any advantage require implementation comparison and evaluation. Motion-Omni ↗
