HUMAN–ROBOT INTERACTION · SEPTEMBER 14, 2026

Giving conversation
a bodily expression.

Can behavior learned from human motion help a small social robot feel more approachable and understanding?

Japanese + EnglishSota experiment preparationResearch in progress

Emotion Bridge connects Qwen’s generated reply and native speech to a learned body-motion model. The next step is to test the movement on the laboratory’s Sota, then evaluate the experience of short emotional everyday conversations.

Software chain demonstrated. Robot evaluation next.
Physical execution and effects on perceived affinity or empathy have not been established.

What is integrated today?

One resident inference system generates an actual reply, encodes its speech with the resident audio encoder, and produces learned body motion. The browser plays the original audio and drives the preview from the audio clock.

What does “unified” mean here?

Qwen supplies the multimodal foundation. Our body generator is an added learned component. This is not a newly jointly trained end-to-end audio, vision, language and body model. The latest integrated test used text input; microphone and vision inputs remain to be verified through this body path.

03 / THE RESEARCH QUESTION

Move from “it runs”
to “it helps the interaction.”

The target is movement adequate for a short Sota experiment. Human-level motion fidelity is not an entry requirement. Further model tuning should address an observed obstacle to that experiment.

01

Borrow the laboratory Sota

Confirm the unit, available SDK/configuration and connection method. Borrowing is planned; access has not yet been arranged.

02

Run a physical rehearsal

Map the learned motion to the actual joint conventions. Check range, speech/motion timing and completion on short dialogue exchanges.

03

Freeze the system for a pilot

Compare the experience under controlled conditions. Preserve failures and unfavorable ratings as research evidence.

Isolating the motion effect

Same-speech development materials are ready: unchanged learned motion versus holding its initial pose. Original speech, timing and starting posture match across conditions. This isolates added movement, not the superiority of a learning method.

Testing the research contribution

A moving procedural baseline is needed for a stronger method comparison. The final short live study should assess perceived understanding, affinity and willingness to converse; its measures, sample and analysis are not yet fixed. Positive effects and publication novelty remain hypotheses.

WHERE THE DIFFICULTY LIES

Integration is necessary.
Useful expression is the real test.

The remaining challenges are matching behavior to the reply, preserving timing and variation through a low-degree-of-freedom robot, and showing that people experience a benefit. A larger jointly trained model is not automatically necessary to answer this research question.
